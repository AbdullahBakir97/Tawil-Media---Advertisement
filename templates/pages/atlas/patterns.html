The Neukölln bookshop keeping Arabic poetry alive

How a family-run shop on Sonnenallee became a meeting point for three generations of readers.

RH Rania Haddad · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

On a grey Tuesday the shop is full. A student from Damascus reads Darwish aloud to a pensioner from Aleppo, and the owner refills the tea without being asked. Half of these titles are out of print everywhere else in Europe, and the shelves are arranged by city rather than by author.

“People come for a book and stay for the argument.”

The shop opened in 2004 with a single shelf at the back of a grocery. Today it stocks four thousand titles.
